03.01 / ophthalmic vision case

Corvis ST Corneal
Image Super-Resolution

We used computational image enhancement to recover clinically useful vertical detail from high-frame-rate Corvis ST sequences.

Organization
Beijing Tongren Hospital collaboration
Year
2021
Role
Algorithm development and image analysis

01

Clinical challenge

Optical distortion and acquisition error limit the vertical resolution of Corvis ST images, which can obscure fine corneal deformation patterns used in biomechanical assessment.

02

Project contribution

We developed a computational method that preserves high temporal resolution while reconstructing additional vertical image detail across the air-puff sequence.

03

Outcome

Evaluation through central corneal thickness measurements revealed previously unrecognized deformation patterns and statistically significant differences between normal and keratoconus groups (P < 0.01).

  • Super-resolution
  • Corneal imaging
  • Time-series analysis
  • Clinical measurement

03.02 / ophthalmic vision case

Deep Learning–Based
Choroidal OCT Analysis

We built an OCT analysis system designed to quantify choroidal change and support objective evaluation of myopia-control interventions.

Organization
The Hong Kong Polytechnic University
Year
2023
Role
Algorithm design and analysis

01

Analysis system

We designed convolutional neural network methods for automatic choroid segmentation and foveal localization, with additional modules for vessel segmentation and choroidal vascularity index analysis.

02

Measurements

Our pipeline extracts subfoveal choroidal thickness, vascularity, and morphological parameters from OCT images so longitudinal treatment effects can be evaluated consistently.

03

Outcome

In the DIMS study, we found that subfoveal choroidal thickness increased by 6.75 ± 1.52 μm after one week, versus −3.17 ± 1.48 μm in controls (P < 0.0001), with choroidal change negatively associated with axial elongation.

  • OCT
  • CNN segmentation
  • Fovea localization
  • Myopia control

04.01 / industrial vision case

Battery Terminal
Inspection Concept

We developed a machine-vision concept for separating localized surface and edge irregularities from the strong concentric geometry of a battery terminal.

Company
Confidential battery manufacturer
Project date
Not publicly disclosed
Role
Machine Vision Engineer

01

Project overview

The inspection sample exposes a high-contrast circular structure with localized texture variation around the outer seam. The project challenge was to preserve subtle defect evidence while suppressing illumination gradients and expected ring boundaries.

02

My contribution

We designed the inspection concept and proposed pipeline: locate the terminal centre, normalize radial illumination, unwrap the annular region into a linear strip, then combine edge continuity and local texture descriptors to identify candidate anomalies.

03

Project outcome

We produced an explainable inspection concept and a validation plan for tuning the defect signal against production samples, operator review, and repeatable quality decisions. This public case omits quantitative production results and confidential implementation details.

  • ROI localization
  • Illumination correction
  • Annular unwrapping
  • Texture analysis
